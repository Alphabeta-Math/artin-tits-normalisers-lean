# Independent assessment of Remark 3.27 (arXiv:2509.21915v3)

Verdict: **Remark 3.27 is false as written if “finite CW-model” has its standard meaning, a CW complex with finitely many cells homotopy equivalent to the stated space.** The rank-two infinite-dihedral Coxeter system, with J empty, satisfies the actual hypotheses and gives an explicit counterexample. This does not affect the spherical-type assertion proved in the dedicated Lean project, nor the finite-type use of the remark in Remark 6.15.

This is an independent mathematical assessment, not a report of an erratum acknowledged by the authors. No article, repository, formal proof, source atlas, or CI input was changed.

## An allowed rank-two Coxeter system

Take S={s,t}, m_st=∞, and J=∅. This is the rank-two affine system usually denoted affine A1, with Coxeter group the infinite dihedral group. The example has finite rank, so no possible infinite-rank interpretation is involved.

Write α=α_s, β=α_t. In the standard geometric representation the bilinear form can be normalized by B(α,α)=B(β,β)=1 and B(α,β)=−1. Thus

    sα=−α,    sβ=β+2α,
    tα=α+2β,  tβ=−β.

Equivalently, the Cartan normalization has diagonal 2 and off-diagonal −2 and gives the same reflection formulas. Set δ=α+β. Both s and t fix δ, and α,δ form a real basis of V.

The real-root set is exactly

    Φ = { ±(α+nδ) : n∈Z }.

Here is a direct verification, including both parities. The displayed set is stable under s and t, since

    s(α+nδ)=−α+nδ,
    t(α+nδ)=−α+(n+2)δ.

It contains α and β=−(α−δ), so it contains their W-orbits as an upper bound. Conversely, st sends α to α+2δ and fixes δ, so the orbit of α supplies all even n; the orbit of β=−(α−δ) supplies all odd n. Negatives are roots because each simple root has its negative in its orbit. This proves equality, not just containment of a useful subarrangement.

## The actual open Tits cone

For a real dual point φ, put a=φ(α), d=φ(δ). Then φ(β)=d−a. The closed fundamental chamber is

    closure(C) = { (a,d) : a≥0, d−a≥0 }.

Every nonzero point of it has d>0. For d>0 let r=a/d. The contragredient generators act by

    s: (r,d) ↦ (−r,d),
    t: (r,d) ↦ (2−r,d).

The images of [0,1] under the group generated by these two reflections cover R: translations by 2 give all [2k,2k+1], and reflected translates give all [2k−1,2k]. At d=0 the closed fundamental chamber consists only of the origin, whose orbit is itself. Therefore the Tits cone from the paper's precise definition is

    Cone = {φ : φ(δ)>0} ∪ {0},
    Cone° = {φ : φ(δ)>0}.

The openness requirement thus causes no failure of the proposed example. For J=∅, Cone(J)°=Cone°.

Every real-root hyperplane occurs: H_n={φ : φ(α+nδ)=a+nd=0} intersects Cone° at d=1,a=−n. Different integers give different hyperplanes, and root sign duplicates give no additional hyperplanes. The arrangement is locally finite in the actual open cone: in coordinates (r,d) with d>0 its hyperplanes are r=−n, and a sufficiently small neighborhood has bounded r-coordinate and hence meets only finitely many integers. Thus the local-finiteness premise of Remark 3.27 is also satisfied.

## Homeomorphism of the literal space (3.26)

Identify a pair of real dual points (x,y) with z=x+i y. This is just a coordinate homeomorphism; it does not replace the two-cone definition by another conventional complexification. Write

    d=z(δ),   a=z(α),   Q={d∈C : Re(d)>0 and Im(d)>0}.

The two cone restrictions are exactly d∈Q. In particular d≠0. The root hyperplane pair H_n×H_n is excluded precisely when

    z(α+nδ)=a+nd=0.

Consequently the map

    F : X_∅^C → Q × (C\Z),
    F(z) = (d, a/d)

is a homeomorphism. Its inverse takes (d,u) to the complex dual point uniquely determined by

    z(α)=du,   z(β)=d(1−u).

These formulas are continuous; their real and imaginary parts have positive δ-evaluation because d∈Q; and for every n, z(α+nδ)=d(u+n)≠0 because u∉Z. They give both inverse identities. The sign u=−n instead of u=n changes no set, since −Z=Z. There are no extra retained hyperplanes or cone inequalities left unchecked.

Since Q is nonempty and convex, X_∅^C is homotopy equivalent to C\Z. For example, fix d0=1+i and contract the first product coordinate by d↦(1−v)d+v d0.

## Obstruction to a finite CW model

For every integer n take the positively oriented circle

    γ_n(v)=n+(1/4) exp(2π i v),  0≤v≤1,

which is a loop in C\Z. For each integer k the continuous map

    C\Z → S1,   u ↦ (u−k)/|u−k|

induces a homomorphism ν_k:H1(C\Z;Z)→Z. Its values are

    ν_k([γ_n]) = 1 if k=n, and 0 otherwise.

The first value is winding number one. If k≠n the radius-1/4 disk about n avoids k, so its boundary is null-homologous in C\{k}, which proves the second value after applying the displayed map. The disk need not lie in C\Z; that is not required for this calculation.

Applying ν_k to a finite integer linear relation among the classes [γ_n] shows that each coefficient is zero. Thus H1(C\Z;Z), and hence H1(X_∅^C;Z), contains a free abelian subgroup with infinitely many independent generators. It is not finitely generated: every subgroup of a finitely generated abelian group is finitely generated. On the other hand a finite CW complex has finitely generated integral H1 by its finite cellular chain complex. Homotopy invariance of H1 therefore rules out any finite CW model for this actual X_∅^C.

## Scope of the correction

The demonstrated defect is exactly the unrestricted word “finite” in Remark 3.27. Local finiteness of an infinite arrangement does not imply that its complement has finite CW type. A Salvetti model with infinitely many cells, or a finite-dimensional CW model, is compatible with this example. I have not independently established the general Salvetti comparison cited in [29], so this assessment does not assert that changing the wording alone proves every other part of the remark.

A safe restriction is: under the spherical-type hypothesis W finite, the finiteness conclusion is consistent with the actual finite arrangement, and the counterexample does not apply. Nor does the counterexample address the finiteness of a quotient: Remark 3.27 concerns the unquotiented complement, and quotienting can identify infinitely many cells or homology classes. The completed finite-W Lean theorem should not be broadened or weakened because of this separate unrestricted-source remark.
