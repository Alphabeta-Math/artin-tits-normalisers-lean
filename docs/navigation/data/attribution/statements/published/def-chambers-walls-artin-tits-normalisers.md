Let $(T,\mathcal H)$ be a hyperplane arrangement in a finite-dimensional real vector space $E$ with its Euclidean topology, as in [def-hyperplane-arrangement-artin-tits-normalisers](item-def-hyperplane-arrangement-artin-tits-normalisers) and [def-arrangement-topology-interface-artin-tits-prerequisites](item-def-arrangement-topology-interface-artin-tits-prerequisites). Thus $T$ is convex and each member of $\mathcal H$ is a linear hyperplane meeting $T$. Give the complement $T\setminus\bigcup_{H\in\mathcal H}H$ its subspace topology. Its connected components are the **chambers** of the arrangement.

For a chamber $C$, let $\overline C$ be its closure in $E$: the set of points every open neighbourhood of which meets $C$. The affine hull of a nonempty set is its smallest containing affine subspace, and its affine dimension is the dimension of that hull. A **wall** of $C$ is an intersection $\overline C\cap H$, for $H\in\mathcal H$, whose affine dimension is one less than the affine dimension of $C$. Two distinct chambers are **adjacent** if they share a wall. The wall is the intersection itself; the hyperplane $H$ is its supporting hyperplane and is not the wall by this convention.
