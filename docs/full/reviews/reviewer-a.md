## Independent check of the mathematical route

The proposed route is sound. For `w∈N_W(W_J)`, take `v∈W_J` so that `d=wv`
has minimum length in `wW_J`. Then `d` still normalises `W_J`. Its inverse
is also minimal: for `z∈W_J`, the element `d⁻¹z⁻¹d` belongs to `W_J`, so
minimality gives `ℓ(d)≤ℓ(z⁻¹d)=ℓ(d⁻¹z)`.

For `i∈J`, set `c=d s_i d⁻¹∈W_J`. Length additivity for `d` gives
`ℓ(ds_i)=ℓ(d)+1`. Additivity for `d⁻¹` applied to `c⁻¹` gives, after
inversion, `ℓ(cd)=ℓ(c)+ℓ(d)`. Since `cd=ds_i`, this proves `ℓ(c)=1`.
A reduced word for `c` using only letters of `J` must have one letter, so
`c=s_j` for some `j∈J`. Repeat for `d⁻¹` to obtain the reverse conjugation
direction. This proves surjectivity on the simple-generator set directly;
there is no finite-set inference.

Choose one reduced word `δ` for `d` and let `β` be its Artin evaluation. The
words `δ++[i]` and `[j]++δ` in each such conjugation equality are reduced,
because of additivity for `d` and `d⁻¹`, respectively. Ordinary Matsumoto
evaluated in the Artin group gives `βσ_i=σ_jβ`. The two conjugation
directions therefore show `βA_Jβ⁻¹=A_J`. Moreover `πβ=d`. For quotient
surjectivity this already lifts `wW_J`; for actual normaliser lifting one
may choose `a∈A_J` with `πa=v⁻¹` and use `βa`, whose image is `w`.

It is essential to use the same reduced word `δ` in both directions. A
positive lift of `d⁻¹` is generally not the inverse of the positive lift of
`d`, and no such identification is justified. This argument needs neither
Artin-monoid cancellation/injectivity nor a spherical Garside or ribbon theorem.
