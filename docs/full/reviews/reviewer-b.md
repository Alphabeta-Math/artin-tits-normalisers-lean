For arbitrary indexing type `I`, arbitrary `M : CoxeterMatrix I`, and arbitrary `J : Set I`, the intended algebraic theorem uses the group with the standard Artin braid presentation, the actual Coxeter group of `M`, and their canonical generator-preserving projection. The standard parabolics are the subgroup closures of the standard generators indexed by `J`. The pure subgroup is the actual projection kernel. The conclusion is that the induced map between the full normalizer quotients is surjective, with kernel equal to the independently defined image of the pure subgroup intersected with the Artin parabolic normalizer. Inclusion of that image is injective and has range equal to the induced map's kernel.

This is **full algebraic short exactness** for the standard presentations, rather than a conditional theorem assuming the missing lifting result. ## Independent derivation of the missing surjectivity argument

Write `W_J = ⟨s_j : j∈J⟩` and `A_J = ⟨σ_j : j∈J⟩`. Closure and the generator projection immediately give `π(A_J)=W_J`.

Let `w ∈ N_W(W_J)`. Choose `v∈W_J` such that `d=w v` has minimum Coxeter length in `w W_J`. Then `d` still normalizes `W_J`, because both factors do. For each `z∈W_J`, the element `d⁻¹ z⁻¹ d` lies in `W_J`. Minimality of `d`, together with invariance of length under inversion, gives `ℓ(d⁻¹) ≤ ℓ(d⁻¹ z)`. Thus `d⁻¹` is also a minimum representative.

For `i∈J`, put `c=d s_i d⁻¹∈W_J`. Parabolic length additivity on the right gives `ℓ(d s_i)=ℓ(d)+1`. Applied to `d⁻¹` and `c⁻¹`, it also gives `ℓ(c d)=ℓ(c)+ℓ(d)`. Since `c d=d s_i`, it follows that `ℓ(c)=1`. A reduced word for `c` using letters of `J` therefore consists of a single letter `j∈J`. Hence `d s_i d⁻¹=s_j`. Applying this to `d⁻¹` gives the reverse generator correspondence as well.

Choose a reduced word `ω` for `d`. Both `ω ++ [i]` and `j :: ω` are reduced, using right minimality of `d` and of `d⁻¹`. Their Coxeter products are equal. **Monoid-valued** Matsumoto therefore gives `σ_ω σ_i = σ_j σ_ω`. It is essential that Matsumoto permits arbitrary braid-satisfying monoid generators, not only involutions; imposing involutions would collapse the Artin target to the Coxeter setting. The reverse correspondence shows `σ_ω A_J σ_ω⁻¹=A_J`, not merely one containment. Thus `σ_ω` is a genuine normalizing lift of `d`.

Choose `a∈A_J` with `π(a)=v` by `π(A_J)=W_J`. The element `β=σ_ω a⁻¹` normalizes `A_J` and satisfies `π(β)=d v⁻¹=w`. This is a genuine lift of every normalizer element, not just a lift into an ambient group. It implies normalizer-map surjectivity and then normalizer-quotient-map surjectivity. The previously verified general kernel theorem supplies the remaining exactness equality; inclusion of its independently defined subgroup image is injective by subtype inclusion.

No circular use of Lemma 2.8, a presumed exact sequence, an assumed normalizer lift, or a finite parabolic longest element occurs in this derivation. Ambient surjectivity alone remains insufficient in general groups, as recorded in the immutable kernel review; the Coxeter-specific reduced-word argument is the additional input.

## Final correspondence, adversarial checks, and limits

The final `quotientMapTo` changes only the spelling of the target subgroup along a proved equality. Because its proof eliminates `B` using that equality and then returns the original quotient map, and because its representative theorem uses exactly `f(n)`, this transport cannot substitute a more convenient homomorphism or shrink the codomain. Its Artin specialization proves the equality by `map_parabolic` from generator images. Both quotient types therefore match equation (1.2).

The source subgroup is the exact image in (1.7). For any representative `a∈N_A(A_J)`, all elements of `a A_J` lie in that normalizer. Thus a coset intersects `P=ker π` exactly when it lies in the range of `articleKernelMap π A_J`. The general existential characterization proves this coset description constructively at the level of existence, not by declaring it equal to the kernel. The left arrow is inclusion of this independently defined image, matching the article rather than an attempted injection from `P` itself.

The original p. 6 descent and kernel correction are preserved. The substantial extra argument is the local Coxeter minimum/Matsumoto proof of genuine normalizer lifting, which fills the terminal-surjectivity step not explicitly supplied in that displayed proof. This is an explicit necessary addition to the source's argument, not a claim that the article's conclusion is false. No counterexample to the full Artin statement is claimed.

The final type printed in the root-owned `full-axioms.log` is universally quantified over `{I : Type u}`, `(M : CoxeterMatrix I)`, and `(J : Set I)`, followed directly by the three exactness conclusions. There is no hidden lift, finite-generation, finite-rank, spherical, or image-equality premise. Helpers' normalizer/minimality premises are discharged by the proof. The proof works for infinite `J` because both generator directions are proved; no inference that an injective self-map of an infinite generator set is surjective is used. Reduced words remain finite even in infinite rank. Empty `J`, full `J`, and the empty indexing type create legitimate trivial boundary cases; none renders the universally quantified result vacuous.

Accurate accepted scope: **a Lean proof of the full algebraic form of Lemma 2.8 for standard presentation-defined Artin–Tits and Coxeter groups, for arbitrary Coxeter matrix and arbitrary standard parabolic.** It is legitimate to say the algebraic lemma is complete. It is not legitimate to say the article's fundamental-group models, their presentation equivalences, their covering sequence, or a topological identification of the pure group have been Lean-formalized. Those are explicit correspondence limits, not assumptions hidden in `lemma_2_8`.
