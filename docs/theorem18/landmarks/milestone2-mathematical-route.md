## Exact target and definitions that cannot be replaced

Assume the actual Coxeter group `W=M.Group` is finite. Use the existing actual
Artin group `A`, standard generators `σ_i`, standard projection `π:A→W`, and
actual generated standard parabolics `A_I`, `W_I`.

Define `A⁺` as the submonoid of `A` generated by all `σ_i`, and `A_I⁺` as
the submonoid generated by those with `i∈I`. These are actual subsets of the
existing group. Alternatively construct the positive presented Artin monoid,
but then its embedding into this actual group must be proved. Defining the
actual submonoid makes cancellation immediate; it does **not** automatically
prove its divisor lattice, positive-word normal forms, or completeness of
positive braid relations.

Objects are the actual associates of the fixed `J`: subsets `I` for which
`W_I` is conjugate to `W_J` in `W`. Original Definition 5.1, PDF p.21, has

```text
Hom_R′(I,K) = {β∈A : A_I⁺ β = β A_K⁺}.
```

Composition is multiplication in `A` from left to right. Equivalently,
`β⁻¹ A_I⁺ β=A_K⁺`; this orientation matters. Identity and inverse exist
because these are equalities of actual submonoids under conjugation.

For each subset `I`, construct its actual longest element `w_I` and positive
lift `Δ_I`. For `a∉I`, put

```text
L=I∪{a},       K=L\{ι_L(a)},
v_(a,I)=w_I w_L,
μ_(a,I)=Δ_I⁻¹ Δ_L.
```

Here `ι_L` is the permutation of the simple indices induced by longest-element
opposition on `L`. Prove that `w_L=w_I v_(a,I)` is length additive, so
`μ_(a,I)` is the actual positive lift of `v_(a,I)` and belongs to `A⁺`.
Prove the atom conjugation equalities `σ_i μ=μ σ_(τ(i))` for a bijection
`τ:I→K`, using the finite Coxeter longest-element facts and ordinary
Matsumoto. Thus `μ_(a,I)` is an actual arrow of `R′`.

This uses original formula (5.3), PDF p.22. To claim agreement with (5.2),
also verify its equality with the formula on the connected component
`[I,a]`: longest elements and positive lifts factor across commuting
components, so the other components cancel. This is not an extra hypothesis.

Define `R` to be the **subgroupoid generated by these actual arrows** and
their formal inverse arrows, with all the same objects as `R′` (Definition
5.5). A generator inverse is an actual inverse element of `A` with swapped
endpoints. It is not generally the positive ribbon in the opposite direction.

At `J`, the values of the loops form an actual subgroup `R_J≤A`. This is
the paper's group with multiplication inherited from `A`. Prove that the
canonical normalizer quotient map restricts to an isomorphism

```text
q|_(R_J) : R_J ≃* N_A(A_J)/A_J.
```

The domain must not be defined as that quotient or as an arbitrary complement.
The corresponding internal semidirect product must use the actual subgroups
`A_J` and `R_J` and their actual inclusions (Theorem 5.6).

For the next landmark, also prove that the actual groupoid `R` has the
complete positive lcm relations (5.8), and that these relations are exactly
the two lifted Coxeter standard expressions of Lemma 5.9, including their
equal ribbon counts. Generation alone or verification that the relations
hold is not a complete presentation.

## What the existing lemmas do and do not give

The completed algebraic Lemma 2.8 lifts every Coxeter normalizer element to
an Artin normalizer and identifies the kernel image of the pure group. It
does not generate that pure kernel by ribbon loops, prove that ribbon loops
have trivial intersection with `A_J`, or establish positive Artin word
normal forms. Milestone 1's actual geometric covering does not supply those
algebraic facts either.

Already available ordinary Matsumoto evaluates two **reduced Coxeter words**
in the Artin group. It does not decide equality between arbitrary positive
Artin words, nor replace the finite-type positive monoid theory needed here.
For example, powers of a generator are not generally reduced Coxeter words.

The case `J=∅` already asks for the actual Artin group generated by its
positive generators, with its complete braid presentation and positive
monoid structure. This is a useful lower bound on the substantive work;
normalizer lifting alone cannot finish the landmark.

## Minimal finite-type positive inputs

A specialization can avoid constructing Godelle's theory of arbitrary
Garside categories, quasi-centralizer classification, arbitrary ν-structures,
and general semidirect products of categories. It still needs proved
spherical Artin positive theory. A sufficient focused interface consists
of the following actual theorems, not assumptions on the final result.

1. **Homogeneous positive degree.** The group homomorphism `A→ℤ` sending
   every generator to `1` is well defined since each braid relation has
   equal length. On actual positive elements its value is a natural word
   length, positive away from `1`. Positive divisibility with a nontrivial
   factor strictly increases this degree. Every positive element of
   degree one is a single actual generator; generators are distinct
   because their Coxeter images are distinct.
2. **Left and right positive divisor lattices.** Prove existence and the
   universal properties of gcd and lcm for actual positive elements,
   and the cancellation/translation laws for these operations. Cancellation
   itself is inherited from `A`; the lattice assertions are not.
3. **Parabolic longest lcms.** `Δ_I` is the positive lcm of the selected
   atoms, on either side. Consequently
   `lcm(Δ_I,σ_a)=Δ_(I+a)` and
   `lcm(Δ_U,Δ_V)=Δ_(U∪V)` in finite type. Prove the usual longest-element
   generator permutations and the required length additivity.
4. **Coprime positive fractions and parabolic support.** If `c,d∈A⁺`
   have left gcd `1` and `c⁻¹d∈A_I`, then `c,d∈A_I⁺`; likewise if
   `c,d` have right gcd `1` and `cd⁻¹∈A_I`. One can prove this from
   uniqueness of coprime fractions plus fraction existence within the
   parabolic and restriction of gcds. Merely asserting that the abstract
   Artin group on `I` embeds does not alone prove the fraction-support
   assertion. This is a decisive missing supplier in the inspected code.
5. **Positive simple divisors.** The positive lifts of Coxeter elements
   are the positive divisors of the full `Δ=Δ_S`; positive divisibility
   between two such lifts agrees with the length-additive Coxeter prefix
   order. The direction from an actual positive factorization to length
   additivity follows from degree and the Coxeter length inequalities:
   if `σ_v=σ_u c`, then
   `ℓ(v)≤ℓ(u)+ℓ(π(c))≤ℓ(u)+deg(c)=ℓ(v)`.

The global square `Δ²` is central, and every signed group word can be made
positive by multiplying by a sufficiently large `Δ²` power. This particular
cofinality can be proved directly: each `σ_i` positively divides `Δ`, so
`σ_i⁻¹` is a positive complement times `Δ⁻¹`; use the generator permutation
under `Δ` to move the powers to one side, and then choose an even power.
No general Ore-category theorem is required for that argument.

These positive lattice/normal-form facts are substantial established
mathematics. A final theorem conditional on them is useful scaffolding,
but does not finish the requested milestone unless their actual proof
dependencies are available and checked.

## Specialized positive ribbon proof

Call an actual positive ribbon `r:I→K` **reduced** when no selected source
atom `σ_i`, `i∈I`, is a left divisor of `r`. For ribbons this also forbids
selected target atoms on the right: if `r=r'σ_j` with `j∈K`, choose the
corresponding source atom `i` with `σ_i r=rσ_j`. Cancelling the final
`σ_j` gives `σ_i r'=r'σ_j=r`, a forbidden source prefix.

Conjugation by any ribbon is an isomorphism of the actual positive
parabolic monoids, hence permutes their atoms. It preserves the distinguished
lcms, giving

```text
Δ_I r = r Δ_K.                                      (R1)
```

This holds for positive and signed arrows of `R′`.

**Primitive outside divisor.** Let `r:I→K` be positive and reduced, and
choose the first atom `σ_a` of a positive word for `r≠1`. Reducedness gives
`a∉I`. Both `Δ_I` and `σ_a` divide `Δ_I r`: the first trivially, and the
second because `Δ_I r=rΔ_K` and `σ_a` divides `r`. Their lcm `Δ_(I+a)`
therefore divides `Δ_I r`. Cancel `Δ_I` to obtain

```text
μ_(a,I) divides r on the left.
```

The positive remainder `r'` is an arrow of `R′` by cancellation of arrows.
It remains reduced: a selected source atom of `r'` can be transported to
a selected source atom of `r` through the primitive ribbon's atom
permutation. Degree strictly drops. Induction proves that every positive
reduced ribbon is a composable product of the actual outside generators
`μ_(a,I)`.

**Closure under multiplication.** If `r₁:I→K` and `r₂:K→L` are positive
reduced ribbons, then their product is reduced. For `i∈I`, write
`σ_i r₁=r₁σ_j`, `j∈K`. The lcm of `σ_i` and `r₁` is `r₁σ_j`: it divides
that common multiple, and its quotient after `r₁` is a divisor of the atom
`σ_j`; it cannot be `1`, since `σ_i` does not divide `r₁`. If `σ_i`
divided `r₁r₂`, lcm minimality and cancellation would force `σ_j` to divide
`r₂`, contradicting its reducedness.

Thus positive reduced ribbons form the concrete positive subcategory
generated by the outside ribbons. This directly replaces the part of
Godelle's general ν-category argument needed here; it does not assume its
conclusion as an interface.

## Clearing signed ribbons and proving trivial intersection

Let `S` be the full index set and `ι` global longest opposition. Put

```text
δ_I = Δ_I⁻¹ Δ_S : I→ι(I),
c_I = δ_I δ_(ι(I)) = Δ_I⁻² Δ_S² : I→I.
```

The first is a positive simple ribbon. It is source-reduced by the minimal
coset representative property of `w_I w_S`: if a selected atom positively
divided its positive lift, degree and projection would contradict the
increase of Coxeter length under left multiplication by that atom.
Consequently `δ_I` is a positive outside-ribbon product by the preceding
induction. Closure proves that `c_I` is a positive reduced loop.

Equation (R1) and centrality of `Δ_S²` give the useful naturality identity

```text
r c_K = c_I r                 for every ribbon r:I→K.       (R2)
```

Every primitive `μ_(a,I)` positively divides `δ_I`, since `Δ_(I+a)`
divides `Δ_S`. Therefore `μ_(a,I)⁻¹ c_I` is a positive reduced ribbon:
cancel `μ` from `δ_I`, then multiply by `δ_(ι(I))`. Induction on a
signed generating path, using (R2), proves that every actual arrow
`r:I→K` of `R` satisfies

```text
r c_K^n is a positive reduced ribbon for some n≥0.          (R3)
```

No formal localization of an unrelated category is being declared equal
to `R` without an embedding proof: (R3) concerns actual products in `A`.

Now suppose `r∈R_J∩A_J`. Write `r=a b⁻¹` with
`a=r c_J^n`, `b=c_J^n` positive and source-`J`-reduced. Remove their
greatest common right divisor `d`, writing `a=a'd`, `b=b'd`. Then
`r=a'b'⁻¹` is right-coprime. Each `a',b'` is a prefix of a reduced
positive ribbon, hence has no selected `J` atom as a prefix. Parabolic
fraction support puts both `a',b'` in `A_J⁺`. Every nonidentity positive
element of `A_J⁺` has such an atom prefix, so both are `1`. Thus

```text
R_J ∩ A_J = {1}.
```

This is the needed injectivity of the actual quotient restriction. It
bypasses a full decomposition of all quasi-central ribbon categories.

## Surjectivity of the actual quotient restriction

Given `g∈N_A(A_J)`, clear signs using a central even power:
`p=gΔ_S^(2n)∈A⁺`. The element `p` still normalizes the **same** `A_J`.
Repeatedly remove selected `J` atoms from the left and right of `p`
while possible. Positive degree termination yields

```text
p=a r b,     a,b∈A_J⁺,
```

where `r` has no selected atom on either end and still normalizes `A_J`.
For every `i∈J`, let `d=gcd_left(r,σ_i r)` and write

```text
r=d c,       σ_i r=d z,       gcd_left(c,z)=1.
```

Then `r⁻¹σ_i r=c⁻¹z∈A_J`. Parabolic fraction support gives
`c,z∈A_J⁺`. Since `c` is a right divisor of `r`, right reducedness forces
`c=1`. Therefore the conjugate is positive and has degree one, hence is
some selected atom. Conjugation is injective on the finite selected atom
set `J`, so it permutes that set. Thus `r` is an actual positive reduced
ribbon loop, and the earlier primitive-divisor induction puts it in `R_J`.

There is no hidden associate-rank claim in this finite-set step: central
**even** clearing keeps both endpoints `J`. Treating arbitrary transporters
with distinct endpoints instead would require proving their atom-set
cardinalities equal.

Use `Δ_S²=Δ_J² c_J` to rewrite the cleared central power as a product of
an `A_J` element and an `R_J` element. Move the factor `b` through the
normalizing ribbon `r` to the parabolic side. It follows that
`g∈A_J R_J`. Since ribbon loops normalize `A_J`, their quotient images
therefore cover `N_A(A_J)/A_J`.

Combining this with the proved trivial intersection gives the required
isomorphism of the **actual** quotient restriction. The internal product
and conjugation action give the source's semidirect product as a consequence.

## The lcm relations and their completeness

For distinct `a,b∉I`, set `L=I∪{a,b}` and

```text
λ_(a,b,I)=Δ_I⁻¹ Δ_L.
```

This is a positive simple reduced ribbon. It is the ambient positive lcm
of `μ_(a,I)` and `μ_(b,I)`. Both divide it since their corresponding
parabolic `Δ` elements divide `Δ_L`. Conversely, for any positive common
multiple `u`, the two elements `Δ_(I+a)` and `Δ_(I+b)` divide `Δ_I u`;
their lcm is `Δ_L`, so cancellation gives `λ|u`.

The standard finite-type two-expressions lemma says `λ` has precisely two
positive outside-ribbon expressions, one beginning at each of the two
different first ribbons. Godelle [17], Proposition 4.19 proves this for
nonempty `I` using positive simple square-freeness and support in `L`:
after choosing the first ribbon, the next outside atom is forced because
the reverse choice creates a repeated atom inside the simple element
`Δ_L`. The source's `I=∅` case must also be covered; it is the ordinary
two alternating expressions for the dihedral longest lift. Do not silently
import Proposition 4.19 with its nonempty-subset hypothesis for that case.

The projection is
`v_(a,b,I)=w_I w_L`. Positive divisibility between simple lifts agrees with
Coxeter prefix order. It follows that this element is the actual Coxeter
join of `v_(a,I)` and `v_(b,I)`: any Coxeter common upper bound has a
positive lift common multiple, which the positive lcm divides. Every
ribbon factorization of the simple `λ` projects to a length-additive
standard Coxeter factorization. Conversely those standard factorizations
lift to it by ordinary Matsumoto. This proves the equality of the two
lifted relations with the relations of Lemma 4.15, as required by Lemma 5.9.

**Equal ribbon counts need a separate proof.** Homogeneous Artin degree
alone does not establish `m=n`, since different primitive ribbons can
have different positive degrees. One may use the finite-type
rank-two/atomic Coxeter lemma identifying the two paths and their equal
counts (original Lemma 4.15 and Corollary 4.13), or prove the corresponding
finite two-wall combinatorics directly. Neither a general Coxeter braid
theorem nor the existing monoid-valued ordinary Matsumoto declaration is
itself this missing atomic-path theorem. This input must remain explicit
until formally supplied.

For **completeness** of the positive relation system, a direct induction
on actual positive degree avoids general Garside-category presentation
machinery. Given two positive ribbon paths with the same value, cancel
a common initial primitive ribbon when they have one. Otherwise their
distinct first ribbons have lcm `λ`; it divides the common value. Factor
the positive reduced remainder into primitive ribbons. Induction, after
cancelling each initial ribbon, joins the two tails to the two specified
`λ` factorizations followed by this remainder. Apply the lcm relation in
the middle. This proves that all actual positive equalities are generated
by (5.8), not just that (5.8) holds.

Then use (R2)–(R3) to clear inverses in two signed paths by the same target
power `c_K^n`. The clearing identities are positive equalities and hence
already consequences of the positive relations. Equality in the actual
group reduces to equality between the resulting positive paths; apply
positive completeness and cancel the added loop in the presented groupoid.
This proves the complete groupoid presentation without merely defining
`R` to be the presented groupoid.

  does not reproduce this invalid reduction.
